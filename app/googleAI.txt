Does Corvinth works but?
Yes, Corvinth absolutely works, but it operates as a filtering tool rather than an active data-deletion program. It acts like an automated digital border patrol for your platform's upload queue—intercepting, blocking, or flagging matching data before it can ever be uploaded. 
While highly effective, platform owners must understand its structural boundaries and operational limitations to deploy it properly:
1. It Blocks Uploads, It Does Not Delete External Data
The Catch: Corvinth cannot reach into the wider internet, Google search engines, or external websites to wipe or take down an image that has already been leaked elsewhere.
The Function: It only protects your platform's ecosystem by stopping users from uploading or spreading that content onto your specific site. 
2. It Depends on Two Separate Detection Pipelines
According to the Corvinth Architecture, the system works through two completely separate pipelines, and its performance depends entirely on which one you trigger: 

www.corvinth.com
Shield (The Hash Pipeline): This matches strict visual fingerprints in 72 milliseconds. It is flawless at catching exact matches, horizontal flips, or mirrored images. However, a standard hash can fail if the image is heavily cropped, deeply edited, or structurally altered.

 +1

Because this requires contextual AI processing, it takes slightly more computing resources than raw hashing. 

www.corvinth.com

3. "Your Policy, Our Detection"
No Auto-Banning: Corvinth does not automatically moderate or ban users on its own volition.
The Output: The API simply hands your server a mathematical signal confirming whether a match exists. Your engineering team must write the code determining what happens next—whether you silently drop the upload, flag it for human review, or lock the offending account.
